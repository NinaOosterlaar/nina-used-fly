# Day 6: Jeju city

We arrived on Jeju airport in the beginning of the afternoon by plane from [Seoul](/blog/south-korea-in-the-fall/my-soul-seoul)
At the airport we could pick up our rental car. 
We booked a rental car at Lotte-rent-a-car. 
Lotte is a big conglomerate in Korea, who is owner of a big portion of tourist accomadations, including rental cars in Korea.
Since it is such a big company, we had confidence in the quality of the service. 
When going outside of Europe, you always have to pay attention to not getting scammed, so that is why we chose to support a big company.
However, next to the counter of Lotte-rent-a-car, there were also some smaller rental car companies.
And while there was a big queue at Lotte, the other companies were very empty, which made me sad...
But nothing to do about it anymore, so we got our car, and started driving for the first time in Korea.
Or specifically, my dad drove for the first time in Korea. 
Luckily, driving in Jeju is more chill, there is a very strict speed limit of 80 km/h at bigger roads, and slower within the city. 
After some confusion about how to drive in Korea, we managed to go to our first destination.

## Dongnum Market

Our first stop was Dongnum Market, the largest and oldest traditional market on Jeju island. 
You can get fresh seafood and meat here, as well as some knicknacks and clothes.
The market was very busy and crowded, but it had a nice authentic feel to it, so we really enjoyed it.
We ate some food here at the market, since we did not really have a proper lunch yet, so we tried some signature black pork. 
Next to the black pork guy, the guy selling dumplings was really eager to get us to try his dumplings as well, but we had to disappoint him.
Logically, on an island, there was a lot of fish for sale, and a lot of the fish were sitll alive as well, which is of course quite common in Asia, but a weird sight for Europeans.
We also got some bread, and other snacks for the next day.
All the people were in general very friendly, even though there is of course a language barrier.

## Exploring Jeju city

After the market, we walked around a part of the city towards the sea. 

### Sanjicheon Street

Right next to the market, the Sanjicheon Street is located. 
Through this street, there runs a stream that is connected to the port of Jeju city.
We were in Korea during the dry season, so the stream did not have a lot of water, but it was a peaceful stroll. 
There were also some art installations you can take pictures with, and a nice couple made a picture of our family. 
Apparently, in the summer it is quite busy with fishermen, since the stream contains a lot of freshwater fish.

# Day 7: Hallasan National Park

To walk one of the two main trails going to the top of Hallasan mountain, you need to make a reservation beforehand.
There are no costs attached to this, there are just a limited amount of people they allow on the trail each day. 
We bought a ticket for the Seongpanak trail, which is the longer and less difficult trail.
Our resevation time was before 8 am in the mroning, so we had to wake up early. 
I chose this early time slot, since the Hallasan National Park actually has deadlines.
You need to be at 2/3 of the way to the top before 11:30. 
The peak closes at 13:30, afterwards they force everyone to leave the peak immediately. 
So there was definitely some pressure to get to the top on time.
And me and my sister are not that experienced in hiking, but we certainlty wanted to try anyways.
Luckily breakfast already opened at 7 am so after a good breakfast, our dad drove us to the trailhead.

When arriving there, we walked to the gate.
You actually need to show your passport with your reservation, so keep that in mind if you every want to do this hike.
But everything was fine in our case, and we could start our adventure.

## Haenyeo Museum

First of all, this museum is insanely cheap.
It is less than one euro entry fee. 
And the museum is really interesting and in my opinion essential cultural knowledge for Jeju island. 
So what are Haenyeo women?
Haenyeo are female divers that dive into the sea to catch fish and other seafood.
They have been doing this for centuries, and it is a very important part of the culture and history of Jeju island.
Centuries ago, the gear they used was very basic, so the job was very dangerous, and these women are really skilled and brave.
This museum was started to preserve their legacy and showcase their culture.

The first part of the museum shows their traditional houses, the househould tools they used, and their everyday clothing. 
The houses were small and simple, as you can see in the pictures. 
The women used garot clothing, which is a form of hanbok. 
The colours of the clothes were brown like the colour of Jeju earth, since it would not look as dirty. 
Additionally, it is easy to clean and it resists decay and odors when wet, which is essential. 
The tools they used is often made from straw and wild grass. 
The items they used are often very practical.
One example of one object they made is the Aegigudeok.
This is a bed-shaped cradle made of bamboo. 
They carried there babies from 3 days old until 3 years old.
Another item is the charong, which is a basket used for storing rice and rice cakes. 
Rice in charong would not easily spoil.

The next part of the museum focuses on how dangerous their work is, and the rituals that they created around their work. 
An old haenyeo saying goes: "Earn from Heaven, spend on Earth". 
This saying reflects the fact that their work is very dangerous.
The haenyeo depend on the dragon god, who controls the sea. 
Thus, they often visit Haesindang Shrine, to hold rituals to pray for safety and abundant harvest. 
In the second month of the lunar calendar, the haenyeo hold Yeongdeunggut, a rite to honor Yeongdeung God. 
They believe this god provides safety and abundant fish, and they believe the god visits the island on the first day of the second lunar month. 
The god leaves the island on the 15th through the smaller island Udo, next to Jeju island.

Next, we moved to the second story of the museum. 
The first section on the second floor shows a replica of a bulteok, which is a circle of stones on the coast that the haenyeo used to rest and gather around. 
The second story also shows their diving clothes, which is still quite basic. 
Additionally, it shows their equipment, the most eye-catching one being their diving nets and buoy, but they also use tools to scrape off sea food from the rocks.
The rest of the second story shows some signs with information and pictures.
There is a sign that explained sumbisori, which is the whistling sound the women make when they surface.
The hoowi-hoowi sound comes from the divers inhaling oxygen and exhaling carbon dioxide built up from submersion in the sea for 1-2 minutes. 
Sumbisori provides fresh air to Haenyeo adn enables them to work extended periods of time with only short rest periods necessary.
A second sign describes Muljil skill. This is acquired by lengthy training and expereince. Haenyeo near the ocean learned how to swim and dive in shallow sea at the age of 8 and became baby haenyeo at age 15.
Physical conditions such as lung capacity, ability to withstand pressure, and capacity to adjust to cold water are needed. 
Cool nerves are also necessary shen swimming near and encountering big fish. Jeju haenyeo ahve extened their skill and wisdom, by learning the effective use of physical strenght and knowledge of the ocean from the seniors at the bulteok.
